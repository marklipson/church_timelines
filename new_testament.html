The first 100 years of the Church are described below, and the books of the New Testament are shown on the right. As you scroll down through the history you will see the Gospels and Epistles filling in. The name of each book darkens when it has been written, and the background brightens as it is circulated among the churches.
AD 30-40
30: Pentecost, the Holy Spirit descends upon the Apostles, about 3000 are baptized.
30-32: The Apostles preach and heal in Jerusalem, establishing the first Christian community around Jesus's teachings.
31-34: The Hellenist controversy takes place and the seven deacons are appointed. St Stephen (one of the seven) is martyred.
32-36: Persecution in Jerusalem sends the disciples beyond Judea. Philip evangelizes Samaria and the coastal region.
36-43: Saul of Tarsus encounters the risen Christ on the road to Damascus, repents, and spends the next several years in comparatively quiet preparation.
33-40: The Gospel reaches Damascus, Phoenicia, Cyprus, Antioch, and other Gentile regions; Antioch becomes a major center of the Church.
35-40: Peter's ministry expands beyond Jerusalem; the household of Cornelius is received.
AD 40-50
41-44 Paul and Barnabus in Antioch, where the disciples are 'first called Christians'.
44 Herod Agrippa I executes James the son of Zebedee, the first of the Twelve to suffer martyrdom.
44-46 Peter is imprisoned by Herod Agrippa, is miraculously released, and departs Jerusalem.
46-49 Paul and Barnabas take their first major missionary journey through Cyprus and Asia Minor, establishing churches and appointing elders/bishops.
48-51 The controversy over circumcision and the reception of Gentiles leads to the Council of Jerusalem: Gentile converts are not required to become Jews.
AD 50-60
49-52 Paul's second missionary journey to Macedonia and Greece; churches are established at Philippi, Thessalonica, Berea, Corinth, and elsewhere.
50-55 The Church becomes increasingly Gentile in composition while remaining rooted in the Apostolic Church of Jerusalem.
50-52 Paul spends much time at Corinth; the Corinthian church becomes a prominent Christian community.
51-52 Paul's earliest surviving letters, 1 and 2 Thessalonians, are written.
53-57 Paul's third missionary journey strengthens the churches of Asia Minor and Greece; Ephesus becomes a major center of Christian teaching and mission.
54-58 Paul writes major letters including Galatians, 1–2 Corinthians, and Romans.
57-59 Paul returns to Jerusalem with the collection for the poor; he is arrested in the Temple and imprisoned under Roman authority.
59-60 Paul appeals to Caesar and is taken to Rome.
AD 60-70
60-62 Paul lives under house arrest in Rome.
60-65 Paul's later letters are written; Christian communities are increasingly established across the Roman world.
62 James the brother of the Lord, bishop of Jerusalem, is put to death.
62-68 Peter and Paul are martyred during the reign of Nero.
64-68 Nero's persecution of Christians in Rome follows the great fire.
65-70 The Apostolic generation begins passing away, while bishops, presbyters, deacons, and local churches carry forward the Apostles' teaching.
66-70 The Jewish War (66-73) and destruction of Jerusalem (70). Before the Roman siege, the Christians of Jerusalem withdraw to Pella in the Decapolis, returning afterward for a time.
AD 70-90
The Church continues evangelizing across Syria, Asia Minor, Greece, Egypt, Rome, and beyond.
According to tradition, Thomas: India and the East; Andrew: Scythia and Greece; Mark: Egypt; Bartholomew: Armenia and the East; Matthew: Ethiopia and the East; Philip: Phrygia; Simon the Zealot: Persia; Jude (Thaddeus): Mesopotamia and Persia; Matthias: Judea and Ethiopia.
The Gospel is increasingly preserved in written form alongside the living apostolic tradition. The Gospels and apostolic writings begin to circulate among the churches.
The Apostle John becomes associated especially with Ephesus and the churches of Asia Minor.
AD 90-110
90-100 The Didache, an apostolic manual for early churches around Antioch, is written.
90-96 Persecution under Domitian takes place in parts of the Empire.
95-96 The Book of Revelation is written.
96-100 The Church of Rome writes 1 Clement to the Corinthians, appealing to the Apostles Peter and Paul and their successors.
98-103 The Apostle John dies. End of the Apostolic Age. The generation personally instructed by the Apostles increasingly assumes leadership: bishops, presbyters, deacons, teachers, confessors, and martyrs preserve the received faith.
107 Ignatius of Antioch is arrested and taken to Rome; on the journey he writes letters to several churches, which give insight into the traditions received from the Apostles.
AD 110-140
110-130 Papias of Hierapolis gathers and preserves traditions concerning the Apostles and their disciples.
110-125 The letters of Pliny the Younger and Emperor Trajan describe Christians as a visible and growing population in Roman provincial society, with established patterns of worship and communal life.
112-117 Trajan's policy toward Christians establishes the pattern of persecution for the next century: Christians are not to be actively hunted, but those formally accused and refusing to renounce Christ may be punished.
115-125 Christian communities continue to multiply throughout Asia Minor, Syria, Egypt, Greece, Italy, and North Africa, while maintaining close bonds through letters, travelers, teachers, and shared apostolic tradition.
120-140 Early forms of Gnostic teaching and other rival interpretations of Christianity appear.
124-130 Quadratus and Aristides offer early defenses of Christianity to the Roman world.
130-140 Justin Martyr becomes a prominent Christian teacher and apologist in Rome.
132-135 Bar Kokhba revolt: Jews are barred from Jerusalem, the Church there is displaced, and the bishopric passes to Gentile Christians under Marcus.
